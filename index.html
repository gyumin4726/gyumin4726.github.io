Research

My research asks two questions about pretrained models. When a model adapts with little data, which parts should change and which should stay fixed? When a model carries a fixed-size state through a long sequence, what does that state keep, and for how long? So far I have studied both with Mamba-based vision models. Next I want to ask them of world models, which keep the past in a fixed-size state and roll it forward to imagine what comes next.

Under review
Pretrained Mamba-based vision backbones can exhibit collapsed timescale banks: Checkpoint-aware state expansion with SpanInit
Gyumin Park, Sangmin Yoon
Under review, 2026

The relative decay timescales of a pretrained Mamba-based backbone can be read from its weights without data. Across nine public checkpoints, some had nearly collapsed timescales while others spread over a factor of a thousand. SpanInit initializes added states to fill the largest gaps those timescales leave.

In preparation
Selective scanning with mixture-of-experts for few-shot class-incremental learning
Gyumin Park, Sangmin Yoon
In preparation for journal submission, 2026

SS-MoE freezes a VMamba backbone after the base session and keeps the classifier fixed, so only a mixture of selective-scan experts adapts to new classes. On CUB-200, selective-scan experts beat MLP experts with more parameters in every session.
