Research

I want to build AI that keeps learning reliably under real-world constraints, and AI that understands how the physical world changes. My work follows these two directions.

Learning under real-world constraints. Deployed models get few labels, a stream of new classes, and conditions their training never covered. I work on few-shot and continual learning, where a pretrained model has to take in new knowledge without losing what it knows, so the question is which parts should change and which should stay fixed. At KIST, I adapt SAM3 to traffic CCTV footage.
Key work: SS-MoE

Physical understanding and world models. I treat sequence models as dynamical systems. In Mamba-based vision backbones, I read the decay timescales of the fixed-size state to see how long it keeps information. Next, I want to bring this view to world models, which learn the dynamics of their environment and imagine what comes next.
Key work: SpanInit

Manuscripts
Pretrained Mamba-based vision backbones can exhibit collapsed timescale banks: Checkpoint-aware state expansion with SpanInit
Gyumin Park, Sangmin Yoon
Under review, 2026

The relative decay timescales of a pretrained Mamba-based backbone can be read from its weights without data. Across nine public checkpoints, some had nearly collapsed timescales while others spread over a factor of a thousand. SpanInit initializes added states to fill the largest gaps those timescales leave.

Selective scanning with mixture-of-experts for few-shot class-incremental learning
Gyumin Park, Sangmin Yoon
In preparation for journal submission, 2026
code

SS-MoE freezes a VMamba backbone after the base session and keeps the classifier fixed, so only an expert module of four selective-scan experts and a router adapts to new classes. On CUB-200, selective-scan experts beat MLP experts with more parameters in every session.
